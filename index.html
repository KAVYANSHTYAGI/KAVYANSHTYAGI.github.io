02

University of Tübingen · Sep 2025 to Present · with Andreas Ziegler

CEGNN: Rate as a Feature, Not a Nuisance

Event-camera output rate depends on scene motion, texture and sensor settings. On N-Caltech101 it spans roughly 26× across recordings and differs by class. Most event-GNN pipelines either rescale timestamps, discarding the rate before the network sees it, or train for rate invariance and lose accuracy on real recordings.

CEGNN keeps the rate and uses it: the log event rate drives identity-initialised FiLM layers in a PointNet++ backbone, trained with a two-stage rate curriculum. +14.0 points on DVS128 Gesture and +6.3 on N-Caltech101 at natural rates, and +34 points on N-Caltech101 under synthetic 1/8× to 8× speed shifts, for 49K extra parameters. Negative results included. arXiv → Code →

03

IIT Ropar · May to Aug 2025 · with Dr. Puneet Goyal

SCAF: Robust Hyperspectral Classification via Optimal Transport

Identified that standard HSI pipelines discard recoverable inter-band correlation by treating bands independently. Recovered this signal using differentiable optimal-transport grouping with balanced Sinkhorn assignments.

Per-group Mamba processors isolate sensor noise to individual groups, converting spectral redundancy into a graceful-degradation mechanism, achieving +11.6% accuracy under 20 to 30% band dropout against Transformer baselines.

05

Machine unlearning · 2026 · PACE with Paolo Russo

Privacy Leakage in Class Unlearning: ADVR & PACE

Segmentation unlearning that merely relabels a class leaves a class-shaped hole in the model's output, and a learned attacker can reconstruct the forgotten mask from the output alone. ADVR (adversarial silhouette removal plus retain replay) is the only method leak-safe on both output and feature surfaces across ADE20K and Cityscapes, while keeping more retained-class accuracy than the baselines. Code →

PACE extends the audit to video: averaging a model's output over a ~2-second clip recovers the forgotten class from every published baseline tested, and a time-shuffled control shows that cross-frame redundancy, not trajectory, carries the signal. A permutation-invariant pooled adversary cuts pooled attacker AP from 93.8 to 18.8 on VSPW (person). arXiv → Project page →

Reflara

A replay-native execution-intelligence platform for autonomous AI agents. Multi-agent systems are often black boxes. Once something breaks, you can't go back. Reflara fixes that by deterministically recording and re-running agent trajectories, reconstructing orchestration state, retrieval influence, and memory evolution over time.

  • Deterministic replay of agent trajectories, every step reproducible

  • Replay-native regression testing for long-running agent systems
